### Fixed

- A busy desk audit lock now says whose it is (#6199). `scanloop run` used to exit 6 with "the audit lock was held for more than 1m0s; another desk write may be stuck" and no lock path or holder, so a live writer could not be told from a stale lock. Every writer that takes the shared `audit.lock` (`scanloop`, `deskevidence`, `deskpost`, `deskrelease`, audit recovery and log rotation) now goes through one `deskkit.AcquireAuditLock`, which records its pid, process start and acquisition time beside the lock. A lock still busy after the bounded wait is refused naming the lock file, the holder's pid, process start and age when the record checks out against the live process, and the exact action: wait for a live writer, or `lsof <lock file>` to find an unrecorded holder. A holder that has exited needs no clearing: the operating system releases its lock, and the next writer takes it and replaces the leftover owner record. The message never suggests deleting the lock file.
- `scanloop run` now reports an unreadable audit store as its own failure. The exit stays 6, but the error leads with `AUDIT-READ FAILURE (not a blind poll — the inbound poll was not attempted)` and the pass report prints an `AUDIT READ: FAILED` line, so a scheduled tick can tell it from a blind inbound poll. A test fails on any non-test Go file under `tools/desk` that opens `audit.lock` outside the helper.
