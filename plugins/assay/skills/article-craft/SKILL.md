# Article craft

Start with a reader and a question. For example, “Can an on-call engineer tell whether a timed-out write took effect?” gives the writer a way to decide which details belong. “Write about timeouts” leaves that choice open.

## Set the brief

Identify what the reader already knows, what is new to them, why the question matters, and what they should be able to explain, decide or do by the end. Infer these from the assignment when possible. Treat an untested assumption about the audience as a hypothesis. Give each article one central promise and choose the genre that can deliver it. Use the [genre guide](references/genres.md) for the seven shapes and their evidence requirements.

For an unfamiliar subject, establish the practical problem, explain the idea in ordinary terms and walk through a concrete example before asking the reader to weigh limitations. A reader already trying to diagnose a failure can start with the symptom. Let the opening earn the lesson, develop it where the evidence lands, and close with an action or inference the reader can make using what the article taught.

## Draft from the evidence

Trace each claim that carries the conclusion to the supplied evidence. This includes qualitative and causal claims. For measurements, keep the population, comparison, denominator and material conditions with the result. Distinguish what a source reports, what the author independently verified and what a worked example illustrates. When evidence is missing, narrow the claim, attribute it or identify the question that remains open.

Put qualifications where losing them would change the reader’s conclusion. Put lengthy queries and calculation receipts in supporting material unless reproducing them is the reader’s task. When an introduction promises the reader can try a tool or method, use the worked example to show their first request or action, the input they can substitute and what to look for in the result. In a tutorial, keep each runnable step with its expected observation. When a figure explains a mechanism, give it labels, units where relevant, and a caption that lets a reader recover the point. Read the article without the surrounding conversation and check that it still works.

## Revise and judge

1. **Check the claims.** Verify quantities, attribution, chronology, causal language and limits. Distinguish planned work from demonstrated behavior. Correct the draft before refining its sound.
2. **Revise the prose.** When rhythm needs attention, use the companion prose-cadence skill on the verified passage. Then cut filler, template headings and repeated conclusions. Check the edited sentences against the evidence again, especially any new subject or verb that changes who did what. Follow a supplied voice guide and preserve quotations. When the brief sets a length or reading-time limit, count the whole draft, including tables and figure text. Protect the passage the reader needs to do the task and the qualifications that keep it accurate. If the draft runs long, cut optional detail or link to its source.
3. **Judge the article.** Use the [article review rubric](references/editorial-calibration.md#article-review-rubric) for a separate reading pass across argument, evidence, honesty about limits, voice, genre fit and audience. Cite passages for material findings. Name the weakest criterion and quote the passage behind that judgment. Explain what it could lead the reader to miss or do, and propose the smallest edit that would repair it. If the passage already serves the reader, say why it can stand. For an excerpt, evaluate only what the excerpt supports.
4. **Try the reader’s task.** When an independent reader is available, give them only the article, figures and audience brief. Ask them to explain the central idea, point to the passage that taught it and apply it to one changed input. Ask where they first had enough information to try the task themselves. For a recommendation, ask which alternative is credible and what evidence would reverse the choice. For a news update, ask what the evidence establishes and what remains unknown. Record any prerequisite they had to supply themselves. A self-check can be reported as such when an independent read is unavailable.

For example, “Every request finished within 100 ms” invites the reader to apply the result to failures too. If the source timed only successful requests, insert “successful” before “request.” Use the [editorial calibration guide](references/editorial-calibration.md) when the same rule keeps rejecting effective prose or accepting weak evidence. Publication remains a separate decision for the author or editor.
