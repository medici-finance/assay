# Personal-executive presence

Use this lane for a long-career technology executive or senior practitioner whose
credibility comes from operating evidence rather than a current product identity.
Typical proof spans several eras of applied AI, platform or organizational scale,
regulated-system controls, and open-source contribution. Treat those as a pool of
evidence to select from, not a chronological resume to replay.

This is a reusable editorial and brand-routing guide, not a named person's biography
or an external file dependency. A project may supply its own factual corpus and
approved visual tokens. Do not invent career facts, personal history, or visual assets.

## Position the person, not the product

The personal-executive surface carries judgment built across roles and operating
contexts. A current product, methodology, or company is one proof surface among
several, not the umbrella identity. A product-branded destination can remain product
branded while a personal post or native clip carries the person's own argument.

Useful proof arcs include:

- applied AI across different technology eras, with the actual mechanism named;
- operational scale and reliability, with meaningful units and dates;
- controls carried from regulated or high-consequence systems into new automation;
- open-source leadership and reusable technical contributions;
- organizational design, modernization, and engineering economics.

Select one arc that earns the film's claim. Do not compress a career into a montage of
employer logos, title cards, patents, or unexplained company names.

## Audience and argument

Write for senior technology executives and experienced practitioners who need to make
a decision, while assuming no familiarity with the current product or its vocabulary.
Lead with the decision, incident, or measurement; retain the technical evidence and
the limit. Executive framing names cost, risk, accountability, ownership, or operating
design. It does not replace substance with generic leadership language.

The film makes a complete argument without a click. A deeper product video, article,
or reference may follow, but it is not required to understand the personal-executive
cut. One specific operating question is valid when it can add evidence or contrasting
practice; promotional funnels and generic engagement bait are not.

## Brand authority and visual treatment

`distribution.brand_surface` selects the identity family:

- `personal-executive`: the person's own approved project-local visual authority, if
  one exists. Otherwise use a brand-neutral restrained editorial treatment and record
  the project's tokens or open visual decision; never fabricate a personal logo, portrait, biography, or product-derived
  “personal” theme.
- `product`: the destination product's current visual authority. Do not redraw it as a
  personal brand.
- `other`: the authority explicitly named by the project or user.

Do not require an external README, career repository, or personal file path. If a
project supplies a factual corpus or a `DESIGN.md`, record it as `brand_source`; the
source is optional project input, not a precondition for using this guide.

The default visual posture is editorial and evidence-led: readable typography, clear
figures, restrained emphasis, and no stock AI imagery or employer-logo montage. It is
a direction, not a universal palette. Native LinkedIn assets still need their own
feed-crop, caption, and phone-size review. Product destination assets keep their own
existing conventions.
