# Timing a supplied performance

Plan speech, thought pauses and visual inspection holds separately. Read the script aloud to test speakability. Label approximate timing in a rough cut. For production, listen to the selected track at normal speed. Align each caption and visual reveal to the measured take. If a diagram needs reading time, add a hold in the scene timeline and regenerate captions and both pictures. Keep the original recording and its permissions with the project.

## Write the narration for speech

For newly authored narration, pair conversational delivery with a spoken-language
pass before script approval. Use everyday verbs and natural contractions, split
dense clauses into complete thoughts, and vary sentence length. Questions can
introduce a real question the scene answers; do not add one mechanically to every
scene. Read the passage aloud and resolve awkward phrasing before adding controls.

Preserve the staged argument, factual quantities, conditions, uncertainty and
consequential limits. Prefer explicit spoken forms for ambiguous acronyms,
numbers and symbols; keep readable written forms in the caption text where
appropriate. Keep delivery instructions separate from the spoken text. Start with
ordinary punctuation; only use expressive tags or SSML when that generation path
supports them. A new scene image need not force a split in a connected thought.

For an existing approved script, propose and review the wording changes before
replacing it in production. Do not let a TTS call silently rewrite the narration.
The audition's two scripts have the same word count, but equal word counts are
neither a rewriting target nor evidence that meaning has been preserved.

When comparing inputs, include the actual prior take and fresh baseline takes
under the recorded original direction. Compare direction alone, then direction
plus rewritten phrasing; keep voice, model and generation settings fixed. Try
more than one take, match playback loudness, and retain scripts, instructions,
seeds/settings and raw take hashes. Equal seeds do not make different prompts
acoustically identical. Record the listener's preference for the actual passage,
not a general quality claim from pace, pitch range, transcription or one example.

When trying a materially different delivery, compare a representative opening and
dense explanation before regenerating the full track. Carry the chosen raw takes
forward unchanged where the script still matches. A selected delivery is not a
blanket approval of future takes: review pronunciation, emphasis and listening
fatigue on the completed film. Record what was actually listened to; ASR, duration
and loudness checks cannot establish subjective delivery quality.

## Make room for the thought

Use natural phrasing first, then add silence where an idea needs time to land.
The selected treatment offers useful starting values, not compulsory values for
every sentence or film:

| Timing element | Starting point |
|---|---|
| Scene lead-in | About 0.4 s before speech |
| Thought boundary | About 0.35–0.4 s of additional silence where the take feels crowded |
| Ordinary scene end | About 1.15 s after speech for the visual to settle |
| Dense or conditional scene end | About 1.45 s, adjusted for the reading task |
| Closing hold | About 2.7 s so the final thought remains readable |

Listen to the take's existing pauses before adding more. Do not pad every sentence
mechanically or count natural silence twice. Locate genuine quiet gaps between
phrases using alignment and waveform inspection; never cut through a spoken word
to insert silence. Recheck the edited joins for clicks and clipped consonants.
Preserve pitch and natural speech speed. If the runtime is constrained, revise the
script with staging or choose where a hold earns its time; do not accelerate the
voice or discard qualifications to recover the old length.

## One measured timing handoff

Record raw take hashes, inserted-pause positions/durations, lead-ins and end holds.
Derive scene durations from the prepared audio plus those holds, quantized to the
output frame rate. Keep speech duration and total slot duration distinguishable.
Retain the independent authored estimates and take-sanity limits; a longer style
does not justify relabelling measured durations as estimates to pass the guard.

After inserting pauses, update word alignment to the prepared audio clock and
regenerate phrase captions from one cue source. Carry the same measured timing
into native fframes and HTML preview, retime music/effect cues, and rebuild any
compiled schedule. Let deliberate gestures retain their own duration, using the
extra time for inspection instead of slowing every movement. State and display a
condition before the motion it authorizes.

Recheck affected seams, phrase reveals, captions, mix, decode, final duration and
player seeking/end behavior. Keep the prior version available for a requested
comparison. Preserve all existing editorial, accessibility and publication gates.
