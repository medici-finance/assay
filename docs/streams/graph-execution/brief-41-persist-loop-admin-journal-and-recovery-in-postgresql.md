## Human decision

The question is which design to accept for the rest of the loop-admin execution journal: the part that keeps, across crashes, restarts and restored backups, what was launched and how much launch allowance has been spent. An earlier merged change delivered the journal's base. A launch intent and its receipt commit together before a child starts, a receipt the journal has lost holds the request as unknown instead of relaunching it, a missing or mismatched operator incarnation holds the journal paused, and no embedded database engine may enter the module. This change proposes the remainder and implements none of it until you rule.

**Ruled: option 1, accept with enforced grants.** The driver's [ruling on #7549](https://github.com/medici-finance/assay/issues/7549#issuecomment-7137047813) chose option 1 for this brief, and a [second ruling](https://github.com/medici-finance/assay/issues/7549#issuecomment-7137425358) approved `DR-graph-exec-41` under option 1 by name. Verify row 8 is the grants row option 1 adds. The text below is the question as it was put.

Under the proposed design, a new forward-only migration adds typed records for admitted limits, usage completeness, checkpoints, referenced artifacts and shared profile reservations, plus an append-only ledger of consumed launches. Admission reads a profile's window budget and the launches already recorded in that window under one database lock shared by every caller, and records the new launch in the same transaction. A missing budget, or a count that cannot be read or verified, refuses the launch. A pacing estimate derived from recorded launches may delay a launch but never refuses one on its own. A restored database never grants fresh allowance: restore provenance supplied from outside the database is compared with the current operator incarnation, and a restored journal stays paused until the operator rotates the incarnation and confirms the old execution group stopped. Passive backup and restore carry an inventory of referenced artifacts with their digests, and a missing or corrupt artifact holds the journal. The test fixture accepts only a loopback server's admin database, drops only databases that carry its own marker, keeps the database password out of every printed value and error, and clears inherited PostgreSQL environment variables.

The choice that needs you is who may change the records that decide how much may still be launched: the consumed-launch rows, and the profile budgets and admitted limits that admission reads beside them. If the database role the runtime connects as can update or delete consumed-launch rows, or raise a budget or limit, anyone holding that role can quietly hand back spent allowance, and no passing test would show it.

Options:
1. **Accept with enforced grants (recommended).** A separate migration-owner role makes every schema change. The runtime role cannot change the schema and may only insert and read consumed-launch rows, never update or delete them. It may only read profile budgets and admitted limits; changing a budget is an operator act under the migration owner or another operator role. A new Verify row is added to this brief, with a fixture that proves the runtime role's ledger update and delete, and its budget and limit writes, are all refused. Implementation then proceeds on this design; independent verification and final acceptance stay separate human-gated steps.
2. **Accept with the cell's application role trusted.** One role does everything, as today. In-place tampering with consumed-launch rows, budgets or limits by that role is declared out of scope and recorded as an open limit, and no grants check is added. Implementation then proceeds on that basis.
3. **Accept with changes.** Name the change, for example a different lock scope, a different restore-provenance source or another grant split. The design record is revised to it and comes back for a ruling before implementation.
4. **Hold.** The item stays at its current status, the design record stays unapproved, and nothing beyond the delivered base is built.

Default if no answer: none — blocks until answered

With no answer, nothing moves: the design record stays a proposal, the item stays at its current status, and no part of the remainder is implemented.

## Task

1. Complete exact-version pgx/Goose dependency-risk records before manifest additions. Reuse the selected libraries and repository disposable-PostgreSQL fixture discipline without importing workflow, its store or domain tables. Retain one engine in the cell database with a separate loopadmin schema, forward-only migrations and version table; explicit migration ownership, no automatic operational DDL. The design record owed at pickup states the database privilege boundary: either it records the cell application role as trusted, with in-place tampering of consumption rows out of scope, or it specifies the grants (no DDL for the runtime role, no UPDATE or DELETE on the launch-consumption ledger, a separate migration owner) and adds a fixture row that checks them.
2. Define typed process records keyed by caller namespace, invocation and owner generation: stable request identity, intent/receipt, lifecycle/cancel state, admitted immutable limits, usage completeness, checkpoints, referenced artifacts and shared profile reservations. Persist intent and the journal admission receipt in one transaction before the provider starts. Provider acknowledgment arrives later and is reconciled by stable identity; separate caller-intent commits remain separate. Do not claim an external provider joins the SQL transaction.
3. Implement atomic shared-capacity admission and fail-closed durable launch-count consumption, scoped to the approved profile and time window, across callers/processes. Unknown, unreadable or unverifiable consumption and missing budget refuse. Restart, backup rollback, database loss or passive restore never create fresh allowance or authorize another unknown request. Derive best-effort pacing from recorded launches rather than a cached count; an unavailable seeded pacing estimate alone cannot refuse an otherwise admitted launch. Do not relax hard limits or confuse pacing with the launch brake.
4. Check independently protected current incarnation against activation before admission and receipt delivery, including key/cell/owner/generation, pause, stop confirmation and restore provenance required by the canonical contract. Mismatch, missing source/record or uncertain old group starts paused. Rotation and confirmed group stop are trusted operator acts outside this runtime. Journal data cannot prove its own freshness after a joint database/control restore.
5. Implement passive database backup/restore with referenced-artifact inventory and integrity checks, preserving spend, limits, pauses, unknown launch/cancel states and checkpoints. Restore cannot resume or launch; absent/corrupt artifacts, incomplete ownership or incarnation evidence hold. No deletion of durable data or operational restore is authorized by this source work.
6. Deliver real disposable PostgreSQL restart/deletion/rollback/restore, transaction, concurrency and incarnation fixtures. An unset explicit DSN fails, never skips; injected fake stores are not the SQL evidence. Loopadmin cannot import `workflow/internal/pgtest`, so it re-implements that fixture's disposability rules and keeps every one: (a) accept only a literal-loopback DSN with user, password and port, the `postgres` admin database, and no settings beyond `sslmode=disable` and a bounded `connect_timeout`; (b) create and drop only a uniquely named database that carries the fixture's own marker, and retain any database whose marker or owner does not match; (c) redact the configuration from `String()`, and keep the DSN password out of every error; (d) scrub `PG*` variables from the test and child environment. Row 7 checks these rules without a DSN. Add the no-embedded/no-workflow closure guard with planted import controls. Prepare the dedicated CI/fixture job and all actual cross-module triggers for human promotion where needed; document exact fixture prerequisites and scrubbed failures.
